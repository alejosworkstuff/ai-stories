# Tone and Voice

## Tone is a set of choices

Tone emerges from concrete choices: sentence length, word register, imagery, and
what the narrator notices. A dark tone lingers on decay, silence, and the gap
between expectation and reality. A hopeful tone finds small light in hard places
and lets characters act with agency. A comic tone relies on rhythm, specificity,
and the surprise of the unexpected precise detail.

## Show through sensory detail

Replace abstract emotion ("she was afraid") with sensory, physical evidence (a
pulse in the throat, a held breath, the cold of a doorknob). Specific, concrete
nouns and active verbs carry more feeling than adjectives and adverbs.

## Consistent point of view

Pick a viewpoint and hold it. Close third person filters the world through one
character's perceptions and biases; first person adds intimacy and unreliability.
Head-hopping between characters within a scene breaks immersion.

## Dialogue

Dialogue should do at least two jobs at once: reveal character and advance the
scene. People rarely say exactly what they mean — subtext, evasion, and
interruption feel real. Trim greetings and filler; start late and leave early.

## Register and rhythm

Vary sentence length to control pace: short sentences accelerate and hit hard;
longer, clause-rich sentences slow time and build atmosphere. Read prose aloud to
catch unintentional repetition and clumsy rhythm.
